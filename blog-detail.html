First-Time Buyers Face Rising Prices in 2026 Market

Apr 10, 2026 8 min read By Tonny W.

Entering the property market for the first time looks different in 2026 than it did even two years ago. Rising prices in many metro areas mean buyers need to prepare earlier, budget more precisely, and move faster once the right listing appears.

Understand the true cost of buying

The purchase price is only part of the picture. Closing costs, inspection fees, and moving expenses can add several thousand dollars on top of a down payment. Building this into your budget from the start avoids surprises later in the process.

  • Down payment: typically 5–20% of the purchase price
  • Closing costs: usually 2–5% of the loan amount
  • Inspection and appraisal fees
  • Moving and setup costs
Buyers who get pre-approved before they start touring homes move with more confidence—and close faster once they find the right one.

Get pre-approved before you start touring

A mortgage pre-approval tells you exactly what you can afford and signals to sellers that you’re a serious buyer. In competitive markets, an offer without pre-approval is often passed over in favor of one that has it.

Work with a lender early, compare a few rate offers, and keep your documentation ready so you can act quickly when the right property comes along.

Lean on your agent’s market knowledge

A good agent can tell you whether a listing is priced fairly, how much negotiating room typically exists in a given neighborhood, and what similar homes have recently sold for. That local insight is often the difference between an offer that gets accepted and one that doesn’t.
